01 / THE MATHEMATICS

From equations to motion.

Second-order spatial finite differences produce the equations used by the numerical solver.

01VORTICITY TRANSPORT
∂ω/∂t + u ∂ω/∂x + v ∂ω/∂y = (1/Re) ∇²ω

Vorticity is transported by convection and diffusion. The spatially discretized right-hand side is denoted R(ω).

02STREAMFUNCTION POISSON
∇²ψ = ω

Streamfunction is recovered from the vorticity field using point Gauss–Seidel successive over-relaxation.

03VELOCITY RECOVERY
u = ∂ψ/∂y    v = −∂ψ/∂x

Central differences recover the horizontal and vertical velocities at each interior grid point.

NUMERICAL PIPELINE
INITIALIZE→RK4 VORTICITY→SOR STREAMFUNCTION→WALL CONDITIONS→VELOCITIES→RESIDUALS

The four-stage Runge–Kutta method uses the lecture's stage coefficients ¼, ⅓, ½ and 1. The browser demonstration holds velocities fixed within each RK step and updates wall vorticity between steps, following a segregated approach.

03 / INTERPRETING THE RESULTS

What the flow tells us.

Explore the physical interpretation and the requirements of the full assignment.

01 /

Reynolds number

Higher Reynolds numbers indicate a stronger relative influence of inertia compared with viscosity, typically producing thinner boundary layers and more pronounced secondary vortices.

02 /

Streamfunction

Contours of ψ are streamlines. Differences in ψ measure nondimensional volumetric flow per unit depth between streamlines; the velocity depends on spatial gradients of ψ.

03 /

Vorticity

ω = ∂u/∂y − ∂v/∂x measures local rotation under the course's sign convention. The moving lid creates strong wall gradients and drives circulation.

04 /

Convergence

The maximum absolute residual detects the worst local discrepancy. The normalized L2 (RMS) residual summarizes discrepancies across the interior grid.
